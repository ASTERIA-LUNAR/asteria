Solar power & battery calculator

First-order estimate

First-order estimate: no temperature effects, dust, degradation, wiring/conversion losses or light reflected from terrain. Power = 1361 W/m² (nominal total solar irradiance, IAU 2015 Resolution B3) × (1 AU / Sun distance)² × area × efficiency × cos(incidence) × visible fraction of the solar disk.

Example values - enter your mission's values. The panel area, efficiency, load and usable battery fraction below are placeholders, not data for any real lander.

At the pole the Sun stays near the horizon, so a flat (horizontal) panel sees it almost edge-on, while a vertical panel turned toward the Sun faces it nearly head-on (power ∝ cos(incidence)). The battery must carry the load through the longest darkness; only part of a battery's capacity is usable, so the needed capacity is load × hours of darkness ÷ usable fraction.

About ASTERIA

ASTERIA (Lunar South Pole Landing Site Navigator) is an entry for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 challenge "Lunar Landing Site Navigator". It shows, for sites at the lunar south pole and any date, when the Sun is above the local terrain horizon (solar power and heat) and when the Earth is above it (direct-to-Earth radio contact). At the pole the Sun skims the horizon, so a few metres of hill decide between light and darkness.

Not affiliated with NASA's ASTERIA CubeSat mission. Not endorsed by NASA. This is an independent hackathon project. It is not a mission planning product and must not be used for operational decisions.

What is computed and what is estimated

  • Computed: Sun and Earth positions (validated against JPL Horizons), terrain horizons from LOLA elevation data, rise/set events, visibility windows and their statistics, the launch-slip heatmap, the illumination cross-check.
  • Estimate (labelled in the app): the power and battery calculator (first-order), Earth-link usability (geometric line-of-sight only).
  • Approximations (labelled): terrain from coarser 240 m data for points you click or type inside the map (less precise than the curated sites; validated, see the Validation tab); a smooth-Moon horizon (no mountains) for points outside the map and the reference sites outside the south pole; single landform-centre points for two Artemis III regions whose boundaries are not plotted; the hillshade map uses synthetic lighting.

Methods

  • Ephemeris: Sun, Earth and Moon positions from Astronomy Engine (VSOP87 and a Chapront-based lunar theory). Moon orientation from the IAU/WGCCRE rotation model. Positions are corrected for light travel time and stellar aberration. Azimuth is measured clockwise from local north (toward the lunar north pole). No refraction (the Moon has no atmosphere). Libration is included automatically.
  • Validation: compared against NASA/JPL Horizons on the angle cases and the event times listed on the Validation tab, plus a blind test on freshly fetched cases.
  • Terrain horizons (curated south-pole sites): LOLA gridded topography (LDEM_85S_20M 20 m/px, LDEM_80S_80M 80 m/px, LDEM_75S_120M 120 m/px), ray-marched in 360 directions out to 300 km, observer 2 m above the ground, lunar curvature included. Points clicked or typed inside the map: the same method in the browser on a 240 m version of the same data.
  • Events (sunrise/sunset, Earthrise/Earthset): the centre of the Sun or Earth crossing the horizon (plus the minimum link elevation for Earth), found by a 10-minute scan (60 min for windows over 30 days) and refined by bisection to 1 minute.
  • Windows and statistics: from the sampled series; windows cut by the start or end of the period count only their part inside it.
  • Animation: the Sky view and timeline step through computed samples only (every 10 or 60 minutes); nothing is interpolated between samples.
  • Launch slip: one series covers the whole slip range; each landing date is an exact slice of it, so slip 0 matches the Explore timeline for the same window.
  • Power (first-order estimate): 1361 W/m² (IAU 2015 Resolution B3) × (1 AU / Sun distance)² × area × efficiency × cos(incidence) × visible solar-disk fraction. Battery = load × longest darkness ÷ usable fraction.
  • Map: polar stereographic tiles made locally from the LOLA DEMs (hillshade with synthetic lighting; slope from the DEM) and from NASA's published average-illumination maps (PGDA product 69: 60 m map for about 85°S-90°S, 120 m map beyond). Same projection as the DEMs, so markers and tiles line up. The map only shows the area covered by the elevation data.

Limitations

  • Points outside the map and sites outside the south pole use a smooth Moon horizon - terrain not included. Real hills and crater walls can change when the Sun and Earth rise and set a great deal (compare the terrain and smooth columns on the Validation tab).
  • Points clicked or typed inside the map use terrain from coarser 240 m data: features smaller than about 240 m are smoothed out (see the Validation tab for how much this changes the results).
  • Terrain masks have 1° azimuth resolution and DEM resolution of 20-120 m; boulders and the lander itself are not modelled.
  • "Earth link" uses Earth's centre and a geometric line of sight; antennas, power, ground stations on Earth and relay satellites are not modelled.
  • The Moon is treated as a sphere of radius 1737.4 km for the Sun/Earth directions. Ignoring a site's height shifts the Earth's direction by at most about height ÷ Earth distance (about 0.001° for 6 km) and the Sun's by far less.
